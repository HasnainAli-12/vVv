# Workflow Taste
- Wants a plan laid out before implementation on non-trivial features ("create a plan first then start working since that's a proper way"; again "plan it out thoroughly" before building a level). Confidence: 0.9
- Expects ambitious, fully fleshed-out results rather than minimal placeholders — explicitly asks the agent not to "just add simple objects" and to build the full course rather than a sketch. Confidence: 0.7
- Delegates creative/design decisions to the agent and explicitly grants autonomy ("do your own setting, use your freedom") rather than wanting to pick options himself. Confidence: 0.8
- Reports in-game visual/behavioural bugs by the observed symptom, in whichever form is convenient — sometimes a screen recording of Unity play mode (a video file), other times a short plain-language description of what he sees on screen (e.g. "the wall run animation plays for a second, then the character just goes out of animation but still wallruns without the animation"). No fixed preference for one form. Confidence: 0.5
- Bug reports are terse one-liners in casual register ("the climb boosts the player up in the sky bro, doesn't even grab the ledge properly") with no repro steps, logs or values — he tests in play mode and describes what he saw, then expects the agent to locate and fix the root cause on its own rather than ask him to reproduce or gather diagnostics. Confidence: 0.6
- Wants sound assets actually produced ("generate SFX sounds proper ones for jump (grunt sound) and other etc") — an earlier "don't generate" reading was a misread; he wants proper-quality SFX generated, not empty slots or thin stand-ins. Confidence: 0.8
- Improves existing mechanics by handing over external reference material (community articles/tutorials and their pasted example code, e.g. a full third-party controller class he "found online" with "take a look at this code") and expects the agent to read it and fold the applicable techniques into the current custom implementation — not to reintroduce a from-scratch rewrite. Confidence: 0.7
- When a feature still isn't satisfactory, prefers to park it and move on rather than keep iterating: asks for the issue to be written down for a later session ("note it down for now we will do it tomorrow") and immediately switches to the next task. Wants unresolved items captured in a persistent notes/TODO file. Recurring pattern: he manages work as day-scoped batches — hands over a long multi-item "todo list for tomorrow" in one message and expects it written up (e.g. a TODO file) rather than worked immediately. Confidence: 0.7
- Rejects band-aid fixes and demands the root cause be handled: when a mechanic misbehaves, wants the underlying behaviour prevented structurally rather than suppressed by tuning/timing ("please fix this properly"). Repeated symptom reports after a tuned fix are a signal that the approach, not the value, is wrong. Confidence: 0.75
- Prefers to sequence the work himself in steps: after handing over a chunk he expects it completed and verified before he gives the next instruction ("first make sure ... then I'll tell you what to do next"). Confidence: 0.6
- Briefs design work as an exhaustive spec — required elements, explicit scale/constraint rules, strict prohibitions, and a prescribed output format — and expects the deliverable to follow that format and include a self-audit confirming compliance (e.g. a final checklist that every required element appears and nothing prohibited was used). Confidence: 0.65
